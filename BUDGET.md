# Kenya’s FY 2026/27 Budget: Where the Money Comes From, Where It Goes, and What It Means for Citizens

Kenya’s FY 2026/27 Budget is built around a central challenge: **the Government wants to fund public services and development while facing limited fiscal space, high debt-service obligations, and pressure to raise revenue.**

The Government projects **KSh 3.6305 trillion in total revenue**, including Appropriation-in-Aid, equivalent to about **17.4% of GDP**. Ordinary revenue is expected to account for **KSh 2.9857 trillion**.

At the same time, the Government plans to spend approximately **KSh 3.5684 trillion on recurrent expenditure** and **KSh 750 billion on development expenditure**.

The gap between what the Government collects and what it plans to spend means borrowing remains an important part of the financing strategy.

For FY 2026/27, the Government plans **KSh 116.2 billion in net external borrowing** and **KSh 1.0301 trillion in net domestic borrowing**. The Budget therefore continues to rely heavily on borrowing, even as the Government says it is pursuing fiscal consolidation and debt sustainability.

---

## Revenue: How Will Government Raise the Money?

The Government's ordinary revenue target is **KSh 2.9857 trillion**.

Rather than relying primarily on higher tax rates, the Budget Statement says the Government intends to broaden the tax base, improve compliance, reduce leakages and make greater use of digital systems.

The Government says there were more than **6.6 million active taxpayers by March 2026**, up from approximately 6.5 million in March 2025.

It also reports that more than **655,000 taxpayers** had been brought onto the Electronic Tax Invoice Management System (eTIMS).

The Government's broader strategy involves digital tax administration, electronic invoicing, taxpayer registration, information sharing and integration of taxpayer data.

The Budget Statement notes that only about **3.1 million working Kenyans currently contribute through Pay As You Earn (PAYE)**. Broadening the tax base is therefore presented as a way of bringing more economic activity into the formal tax system.

For ordinary Kenyans, this means that the tax conversation is not simply about whether tax rates increase or decrease. The effect will also depend on which goods, services, businesses and economic activities become subject to taxation or closer compliance monitoring.

---

# What Happens to Taxes?

The Finance Bill contains a mixture of tax increases, reductions, exemptions, customs changes and administrative reforms.

The Government says its guiding principles are **efficiency, fairness and simplicity**.

Some of the proposed changes include removing the **KSh 6.41 per litre excise duty on bottled water**.

For mobile phones, the Bill proposes replacing multiple taxes and levies with a **single 25% excise duty**.

There are also proposed increases in excise duty on certain tobacco products. For example, manufactured tobacco, tobacco substitutes, extracts and essences would increase from **KSh 11,382.48 per kilogram to KSh 12,550 per kilogram**, while certain cigars, cheroots and cigarillos would increase from **KSh 16,260.29 per kilogram to KSh 18,000 per kilogram**.

The Bill also proposes a **5% excise duty on coal**.

Certain alcoholic beverages manufactured by small independent brewers would lose their preferential excise-duty treatment.

Gambling, lottery and prize-competition winnings would become subject to withholding tax.

At the same time, the Government says it is **not proposing an increase in monthly rental income tax**. Instead, proposed changes concern non-resident persons earning rental income in Kenya and seek to simplify the framework.

For passengers entering Kenya, the proposed VAT-exempt threshold for goods carried through ports of entry would increase from **USD 300 to USD 2,000**.

Goods and services imported or procured locally for use solely in approved PPP infrastructure projects would also receive a proposed VAT exemption.

---

# Debt: Kenya Is Still Borrowing

The FY 2026/27 Budget does not represent an end to Government borrowing.

The Government describes debt as a development tool that should be used prudently. Its stated approach is to combine **fiscal consolidation, debt sustainability, diversification of financing sources and better debt management**.

The Government is also examining alternative international financing instruments.

These include potential access to **Samurai Bonds** in Japan and **Panda Bonds** in China.

The Government also proposes exploring **Sharia-compliant Sukuk instruments**, which could expand the pool of investors able to participate in Kenyan government financing.

---

# Why Does the Government Say Fiscal Space Is Limited?

The Budget Statement identifies several pressures on public finances.

These include:

* rising public-sector wage costs;
* emergency expenditure arising from floods and drought;
* revenue pressures;
* debt-service obligations; and
* continuing demand for essential public services.

The Government therefore says it must prioritise expenditure and look for alternative financing mechanisms.

One of the most significant elements of this strategy is greater use of private capital through **Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs)**.

---

# PPPs: Bringing Private Money Into Public Projects

The Government estimates Kenya's infrastructure financing gap at approximately **USD 5 billion every year**.

Its position is that taxation and Government borrowing alone cannot finance all the infrastructure the country requires.

The Government therefore wants to attract capital from:

* pension funds;
* banks;
* private equity;
* development-finance institutions;
* private investors; and
* other sources of long-term capital.

The Government is targeting at least **KSh 70 billion in PPP investments during FY 2026/27**.

The targeted areas include **energy, transport, water, housing, health and digital infrastructure**.

The Government established the **National Infrastructure Fund in March 2026** to help mobilise long-term capital from pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, private equity, banks and development-finance institutions.

The Government also proposes using proceeds from the **privatisation of mature national assets** as a source of funding for the Fund.

---

# Agriculture: Fertiliser, Seeds and Food Production

Agriculture remains a major part of the Government's strategy for reducing production costs and improving food security.

The Budget proposes:

* **KSh 18 billion** for the Fertilizer Subsidy Programme;
* **KSh 2 billion** for the Seed Subsidy Programme; and
* **KSh 1 billion** for the Coffee Seedlings Programme.

The Government expects these interventions to reduce input costs and increase agricultural productivity.

However, cheaper agricultural inputs do not automatically translate into cheaper food. Food prices are also affected by weather, transport, storage, fuel costs, market structures, imports, exchange rates and post-harvest losses.

The Budget Statement cites increased agricultural production as evidence of progress. It reports that maize production rose to approximately **67 million bags in 2025 from 34 million bags in 2022**, while maize imports fell from approximately **9.9 million bags to 3.3 million bags** over the same period.

These figures are presented by the Government in the Budget Statement.

---

# Coffee Farmers

The Budget proposes several measures targeting the coffee sector.

These include:

* **KSh 1 billion** for the Coffee Seedlings Programme;
* **KSh 500 million** for the Coffee Cherry Revolving Fund; and
* **KSh 2 billion** for coffee debt waivers.

The Government also highlights reforms at the Nairobi Coffee Exchange intended to improve price discovery and allow farmers greater participation in coffee sales.

---

# Livestock and Pastoral Communities

The Budget proposes:

* **KSh 1.3 billion** for the Kenya Livestock Commercialization Programme; and
* **KSh 400 million** for the Livestock Value Chain Support Project.

The Government says these investments are intended to improve market access, commercialisation, animal health and resilience among pastoral communities.

---

# Healthcare: KSh 177.2 Billion

The health sector receives a proposed **KSh 177.2 billion**.

The Government says this funding will support Universal Health Coverage and strengthen essential health services.

Within the allocation, the Budget proposes:

* **KSh 19.1 billion** for the Primary Healthcare Fund;
* **KSh 8.6 billion** for salaries for Universal Health Coverage staff;
* **KSh 20.9 billion** for the Kenya Medical Supplies Agency (KEMSA); and
* **KSh 3.1 billion** for the Kenya Medical Research Institute (KEMRI).

The Government says these investments will strengthen health services, medical supply chains and health-sector capacity.

### Cancer Treatment

Cancer-related infrastructure also receives funding.

The proposals include:

* **KSh 300 million** to strengthen cancer management at Kenyatta National Hospital;
* **KSh 150 million** for expansion of the Comprehensive Cancer Centre at Kenyatta University Teaching, Referral & Research Hospital; and
* funding for construction of a cancer centre at Kisii Level 5 Hospital.

### Dialysis

The Finance Bill proposes a **VAT exemption for dialyzers**.

The Government says this is intended to reduce their cost and make renal treatment more affordable.

The actual effect on what patients pay would depend on how savings from the tax exemption move through the supply chain.

---

# Education: KSh 784.5 Billion

Education receives one of the largest sectoral allocations, with a proposed **KSh 784.5 billion**.

The proposed allocations include:

* **KSh 424.3 billion** for primary education;
* **KSh 54.6 billion** for Free Day Secondary Education;
* **KSh 30.7 billion** for Junior Secondary School capitation;
* **KSh 9.9 billion** for national examinations; and
* **KSh 3 billion** for the School Feeding Programme.

The Government also reports construction of more than **23,000 classrooms** and commencement of approximately **1,600 laboratories**, with participation from NG-CDF.

For higher education, the Statement says **177,686 student beds** have been packaged for development across universities, with **354 already under implementation**.

---

# Youth and Employment

Youth unemployment remains identified as a major challenge.

One of the programmes highlighted in the Budget is the **NYOTA Programme**, which provides business-skills training, mentorship, start-up capital and on-the-job experience.

The Government says more than **KSh 5.6 billion** has been invested through NYOTA and that **91,253 young people** have benefited from start-up grants.

The wider employment strategy includes:

* youth grants;
* MSME financing;
* infrastructure;
* manufacturing;
* agriculture;
* tourism;
* digital-economy initiatives;
* PPP projects; and
* private-sector investment.

The approach is therefore not limited to direct Government employment. The Government intends to create employment through broader economic activity and private-sector investment.

---

# Supporting Small and Informal Businesses

The Budget recognises the importance of small and informal enterprises to employment.

It specifically identifies:

* informal home-based enterprises;
* artisans;
* mechanics;
* tailors;
* carpenters;
* fabricators; and
* medium-sized firms.

Affordable credit is also identified as an important constraint facing MSMEs and households.

The Government proposes measures intended to improve access to finance, strengthen competition within the financial sector and expand digital financial services.

---

# Housing and Informal Settlements

The Affordable Housing Programme remains a priority.

The Government presents the programme not only as a housing initiative but also as a way of creating employment and stimulating local manufacturing through demand for construction materials and services.

For informal settlements, the Budget proposes **KSh 2.7 billion for the Kenya Informal Settlement Improvement Project Phase II**.

There is also **KSh 535 million** for construction of county headquarters.

The housing programme is presented as providing affordable homes and improving living conditions, although eligibility, affordability, financing arrangements and geographical distribution determine who can actually benefit.

---

# Roads and Infrastructure

The Budget proposes **KSh 220.4 billion for development of roads**.

This includes:

* **KSh 44.3 billion** for construction of roads and bridges;
* **KSh 58 billion** for rehabilitation; and
* **KSh 118.1 billion** for road maintenance.

Infrastructure spending is closely connected to the Government's broader strategy of using PPPs and private investment to supplement public resources.

---

# Counties: KSh 444.6 Billion

County governments are proposed to receive **KSh 428 billion in shareable revenue**.

With an additional **KSh 16.6 billion**, the total allocation to county governments is projected at **KSh 444.6 billion**.

The Budget also proposes **KSh 57.4 billion in additional allocations**.

County financing matters because many services experienced directly by citizens are devolved or county-linked, including:

* health facilities;
* local roads;
* water services;
* markets;
* agricultural extension;
* sanitation; and
* local development.

The Statement also refers to a **County Governments' Pending Bills Action Plan**, with county governments expected to customise and implement their own plans to progressively reduce pending bills.

---

# The Equalisation Fund

The Budget proposes **KSh 10.3 billion for the Equalisation Fund**.

The Fund is intended to support projects in marginalised areas.

---

# Pending Bills: KSh 155.3 Billion

The Government says the Pending Bills Verification Committee reviewed **91,911 pending bills**.

The verified outstanding balance is approximately **KSh 155.3 billion**.

The proposed strategy for clearing these obligations combines direct budgetary allocations and securitisation.

The Government proposes settling **KSh 68 billion** of claims.

According to the Statement, this would settle approximately **99% of verified claims by number**, representing about **63% of their total value**.

The remaining approximately **KSh 88 billion** would be addressed through other budgetary provisions and instruments.

The difference between the percentage of claims and the percentage of value is important: settling 99% of claims does not mean paying 99% of the money owed, because some claims are much larger than others.

---

# Procurement and Public Money

The Government is continuing the rollout of the **e-Government Procurement system (e-GP)**.

The Budget Statement reports:

* **1,543 procuring entities registered**;
* more than **40,000 suppliers registered**;
* more than **22,000 staff trained**;
* more than **12,000 suppliers and contractors trained**;
* **652 annual procurement plans published**;
* approximately **8,000 tenders published**; and
* **1,863 contracts published**.

Digital procurement can improve documentation, visibility and monitoring, but digitisation by itself does not eliminate corruption or poor procurement.

The Statement acknowledges implementation challenges, including resistance from users and litigation.

---

# Government Assets

Government Ministries, Departments and Agencies collectively hold assets valued at approximately **KSh 4.9 trillion**, according to the Budget Statement.

The Government says better asset management is necessary to improve transparency and accountability.

This includes improving asset registers and management systems so that the Government can better track what public assets exist, where they are located, how they are being used and how they are performing.

---

# State Corporations

The Government is also undertaking reforms of State corporations.

The proposed reforms include:

* mergers;
* dissolutions;
* transfer of functions;
* declassification of selected State corporations; and
* governance changes.

The Statement says a committee considered **23 mergers, 16 dissolutions and seven transfers of functions** back to Ministries, Departments and Agencies.

Under the Government Owned Enterprises framework, Government-owned enterprises are expected to operate as commercial, self-financing, self-sustaining and publicly accountable entities.

The stated objective is to reduce fiscal pressure and improve performance.

---

# Security

The Budget proposes:

* **KSh 252.1 billion** for Defence;
* **KSh 144.7 billion** for the National Police Service;
* **KSh 64.1 billion** for the National Intelligence Service; and
* **KSh 63.9 billion** for Internal Security and National Administration.

There is also funding proposed for village elders.

The Government describes security and stability as essential for investment, trade, economic growth and delivery of public services.

---

# Digital Economy

The Budget continues to invest in digital infrastructure and services.

Proposed allocations include:

* **KSh 4.3 billion** for the Kenya Digital Economy Acceleration Project;
* **KSh 1.3 billion** for maintenance and rehabilitation of the National Optic Fibre Backbone Infrastructure;
* **KSh 528 million** for Last Mile County Connectivity; and
* **KSh 309 million** for Government Shared Services.

The Government also links local assembly of smart devices to its Digital Superhighway agenda.

The objectives include expanding digital access, supporting local manufacturing, creating jobs and developing local value chains.

---

# Water and Climate Change

Water infrastructure receives significant proposed funding.

The Budget provides:

* **KSh 51.5 billion** for water and sewerage infrastructure;
* **KSh 6.3 billion** for water-resources management; and
* **KSh 2.5 billion** for water storage and flood control.

Climate and environmental programmes include:

* **KSh 8.9 billion** for the Kenya Financing Locally Led Climate Action Project;
* **KSh 4.7 billion** for environmental management and protection; and
* **KSh 1.8 billion** for meteorological services.

The Government is also developing a **Disaster Risk Financing Strategy for 2026–2030**.

---

# Social Protection

Approximately **1.2 million vulnerable Kenyans** are currently supported under the **Inua Jamii Programme**, according to the Statement.

The Budget continues to prioritise social safety nets.

For people living with severe disabilities, the proposed allocation includes **KSh 1.5 billion in cash transfers**.

There is also:

* **KSh 550 million** for the National Fund for the Disabled of Kenya; and
* **KSh 4.3 billion** for the Kenya Hunger Safety Net Programme.

---

# Women and Economic Inclusion

The Budget proposes:

* **KSh 402 million** for the Women Enterprise Fund; and
* **KSh 4.9 billion** for the National Government Affirmative Action Fund.

These programmes are intended to support women's economic participation and inclusion.

---

# Financial Sector Reforms

Commercial banks are facing progressively higher minimum core-capital requirements.

The Government says the minimum requirement is being increased from **KSh 1 billion to KSh 10 billion by December 2029**, with the transition taking place gradually.

The Government argues that stronger bank capital can support financial stability.

The Budget also addresses virtual assets.

Kenya has enacted a legal framework for **Virtual Asset Service Providers**, while the Finance Bill proposes a **Virtual Asset Reporting Framework** to improve visibility of virtual-asset transactions.

---

# Insurance and Agriculture

The Statement reports that more than **11 million lives were covered by microinsurance as of 31 December 2025**.

It also reports the licensing of five microinsurance companies during FY 2024/25.

In agriculture, the Government has initiated amendments to the Insurance Act to establish **agricultural insurance as a standalone class of insurance business**, with the stated objective of strengthening agricultural risk management and supporting food security.

---

# Pension Payments

The Budget Statement identifies delays and incomplete submissions as problems affecting pension processing.

It proposes requiring Ministries, Departments and Agencies to initiate pension claims **at least nine months before an officer's retirement date**.

The aim is to reduce delays in processing retirement benefits.

---

# Modernising Government's Financial Management

The Government is transitioning from cash accounting towards **accrual-based accounting**.

The transition is expected to take **three years**.

Accrual accounting provides a broader picture of Government finances by accounting for assets, liabilities, inventories, receivables, prepayments and other financial obligations.

The Government is also implementing a **Treasury Single Account** framework intended to improve visibility and management of Government cash, reduce idle funds, strengthen expenditure controls and ensure public money is released against verified and approved obligations.

---

# The Bigger Picture

The FY 2026/27 Budget is ultimately a story about **balancing competing demands with limited resources**.

The Government wants to fund major public services and development programmes while also managing debt, expanding the tax base and attracting private capital.

The numbers are substantial.

The Budget proposes:

* **KSh 784.5 billion** for education;
* **KSh 177.2 billion** for health;
* **KSh 220.4 billion** for roads;
* **KSh 18 billion** for fertiliser subsidies;
* **KSh 444.6 billion** in total county allocations before additional allocations;
* and a target of at least **KSh 70 billion in PPP investment**.

At the same time, the Government expects to raise **KSh 3.6305 trillion in total revenue** and plans more than **KSh 1 trillion in net domestic borrowing**.

The Budget therefore contains two stories at once: **a story of investment in public services and economic activity, and a story of continued pressure on public finances.**

For citizens, the most important distinction is between **what is budgeted, what is actually spent, and what is ultimately delivered**.

A budget allocation is a plan. Actual releases and expenditure show implementation. Completed projects and measurable improvements in services show outcomes.

That distinction provides the clearest way to follow the FY 2026/27 Budget throughout the financial year.
